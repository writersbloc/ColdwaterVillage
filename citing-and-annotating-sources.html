Step 1

Practice on a shared example

About 15 minutes

Before you work on your own sources, practice on one everyone can see: the website of watercolor artist Rachel Collins. Open rachelcollinsart.com in a new tab.

A. Cite one page from the site

On her site, click Blog and open the post "Classes are starting up!" Fill in each element from that post, then check your work.

Author Last, First. "Title of Page." Name of Website, Publisher, Day Month Year, URL.

Type each element exactly as it should appear in your citation, punctuation and quotation marks included. Use the I button to italicize any element that needs it.

    Model citation

    Collins, Rachel. “Classes Are Starting Up!” Rachel Collins Art, 10 Apr. 2022, www.rachelcollinsart.com/post/classes-are-starting-up.

    MLA capitalizes the major words in a title, even when the site doesn't. The footer's © 2023 is the copyright for the whole site, so it never stands in for the post's date.

    B. Sort three summaries of the site

    A summary in an annotated bibliography tells your reader two things in two or three sentences of your own words. What the source says: its main claim, advice, or finding. What the source does: shows a technique, makes an argument, tells a story, compares options.

    C. Read a model annotation

    Here's a full annotation for the site, with each part marked. Yours in Step 5 will follow the same pattern.

    Collins, Rachel. Rachel Collins Art, www.rachelcollinsart.com. Accessed .

    Virginia watercolorist and scientific illustrator Rachel Collins uses her site to show her paintings of plants, shells, bones, and landscapes and to advertise her classes. In her artist statement she argues that painting nature's forms closely can help viewers notice design in ordinary things. Because this is her own site, the claims about her awards and exhibitions come from her. Her blog says she teaches at the Art League, which a reader could confirm by searching the Art League's own site. For an investigation of watercolor, the site shows what one working painter's practice includes: the subjects she returns to, the teaching she does, and why she chose transparent watercolor.

    SummaryCredibilityUse

    This one cites the whole site instead of one page. A whole site has no page title, so its name goes in italics first. The homepage has no date, so the citation ends with the day you visited.
